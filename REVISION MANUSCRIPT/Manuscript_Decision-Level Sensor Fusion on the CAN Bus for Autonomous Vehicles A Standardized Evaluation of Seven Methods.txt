Decision-Level Sensor Fusion on the CAN Bus for Autonomous Vehicles: A Standardized Evaluation of Seven Methods

Abstract
Purpose - To evaluate decision-level sensor fusion for autonomous vehicles on a CAN bus using one standardized workflow, and to identify models that best balance accuracy, responsiveness, and safety.
Design/methodology/approach - Seven fusion methods (Rule-Based, Bayesian, Dempster-Shafer, XGBoost, SmallMLP, Temporal Sequence, Hybrid) are tested in a deterministic simulator across 15 scenarios with identical timestamped inputs from LiDAR, two cameras, and an ultrasonic sensor. Metrics include precision, recall, F1, mean time-to-detect (MTTD), Full_Brake false-negative rate (FNR), and near-miss rate (NMR).
Findings - SmallMLP yields the highest overall F1 and fast responses. The hybrid achieves low missed Full_Brake rates with competitive latency through explicit safety overrides. Dempster-Shafer is strongest under uncertain or conflicting evidence. XGBoost excels on Slowdown and gives the lowest collision rates in dynamic object scenarios but lags when obstacles appear suddenly. The sequence model is polarized, and the rule-based baseline is fast but brittle.
Research limitations/implications - Results are simulation based and intermediate actions remain challenging; on-road validation, data balancing, and sensor extensions are needed.
Originality - A single CAN centred pipeline with common inputs and outputs, deterministic timing, safety checks, and auditable logging enables fair, scenario-based decision-level comparisons with safety focused metrics.
Keywords CAN bus; autonomous vehicles; sensor fusion; decision-level fusion; safety; SmallMLP; Dempster-Shafer; XGBoost; hybrid model; mean time-to-detect; false-negative rate; collision avoidance

1. Introduction 
Recent advances span rule-based, probabilistic, evidential, machine learning, temporal, and hybrid fusion, while the CAN bus remains central for real-time integration and safety critical decisions. Motivated by time-triggered, endpoint-based interfaces that enable predictable delays and strict synchronization on CAN-like networks (Jiang et al., 2019), we enforce deterministic scheduling, identical actuator mapping, and reproducible logging to benchmark heterogeneous decision logics head-to-head. Seven decision modules (Rule-Based (RB), Bayesian (BF), Dempster-Shafer (DS), XGBoost (XGB), SmallMLP (SMLP), Temporal Sequence (TS), and Hybrid (HB)) consume synchronized LiDAR, dual front cameras, and rear ultrasonic inputs and emit one of four CAN encoded actions {0x00, 0x01, 0x02, 0x03}. Across 15 scripted scenarios, we report per class F1, safety critical Full_Brake false-negative rate (FNR), mean time-to-detect (MTTD).
   Gap and contribution. Prior work often optimises isolated stacks (perception or IDS) or mixes sensors/models without a plug-compatible decision interface, leaving message-level reliability under-explored and head-to-head tests rare. We provide control-plane parity: seven modules share one CAN-synchronized pipeline, a single actuation map, and a common logging schema, producing identical decision bytes for the same timestamped inputs. This enables apples-to-apples comparisons on per-class F1, Full_Brake FNR, and MTTD across 15 scenarios, and directly complements decision?fusion directions in CAN security, such as conflict?aware fusion with mitigation (Moradi et al., 2024).

2. Related Works
Endpoint based, time-triggered CAN interfaces enable deterministic latency and synchronous operation, making them well suited for evaluating decision fusion under fixed update rates and hard synchronization (Jiang et al., 2019). Deterministic timing supports controlled comparisons of models that share inputs, actuation, and scheduling, so observed differences can be attributed to the fusion method rather than timing variability. A comprehensive review unifies model based, signal based, and data driven vehicle health monitoring across conventional, electric, and autonomous vehicles, reporting up to 95% accuracy for early mechanical fault detection, 15 to 20% energy efficiency gains and 10 to 15% lifespan extension for EV batteries, and 99% decision reliability in AVs (Hossain et al., 2025). It surfaces gaps in heterogeneous data integration, edge efficiency, transparent AI diagnostics, and V2V enabled detection, motivating modular decision-level fusion evaluated under deterministic timing.
   Security sensitive CAN settings adopt multi detector aggregation with feature ranking, autoencoders, LSTM based estimation, and Yager’s rule to manage detector conflict, illustrating how evidential and decision-level fusion enhance resilience against adversarial or anomalous traffic (Moradi et al., 2024). As a modular alternative to perception-centric end-to-end stacks, decision-level fusion aligns with CAN timed interfaces. Multimodal RGB and event fusion with attention improves lateral control prediction (Munir et al., 2023), while lean, CAN integrated AV prototypes show cost effective stacks with limited sensor suites (Azam et al., 2020), highlighting accuracy, latency, and interpretability trade-offs that favour compact, interface aligned modules. Explainability remains central in safety critical contexts: rule-based approaches provide transparent logic and auditability (Sarker et al., 2024), and interpretable controllers such as interval type-2 fuzzy decision making and ANFIS based reliability estimation combine explicit rules with learnable components to bound risk (Abdullah et al., 2018; Tyagi & Sharma, 2014). Under conflicting evidence, Dempster-Shafer theory with conflict aware divergence and credibility weighting stabilizes combination and supports principled uncertainty handling; discounting and pignistic transforms remain standard for downstream decisions and enable fair comparisons with Bayesian, tree based, and neural methods (H. Wang et al., 2021).
   Cross domain syntheses report that multi sensor fusion advances state estimation and scene perception across platforms (Du et al., 2025). Outside road vehicles, gradient boosted trees are strong baselines in manufacturing QA (Chuenmee et al., 2025), and attention aided temporal fusion improves welding state prediction (Cheng et al., 2025), motivating evaluation of tree ensembles, sequence models, and compact MLPs for decision fusion on structured, low dimensional signals. Efficiency considerations point to compact MLPs performing well on tabular features (F. Wang et al., 2025) and to digital twin architectures that structure acquisition, modelling, and services for reproducibility (Y. Wang et al., 2025). Scenario based runtime monitoring with Traffic Sequence Charts provides online verdicts in simulators and aligns with deterministic timing evaluations (Stemmer et al., 2025). Robust perception in adverse weather and rail scenes shows that combining modalities and robust detectors aids domain transfer and uncertainty tolerance (Nan et al., 2025; Thottempudi et al., 2025). Work on GNN message passing focuses on throughput and memory efficiency for real-time workloads, aligning with lightweight decision fusion compatible with CAN timed execution (Zhang et al., 2025).

3.4 Fusion Methods Comparison
Table 1 summarizes each model’s fusion logic in one view. RB is deterministic;
BF updates posteriors; DS combines beliefs; XGB outputs calibrated tree scores; SMLP and TS produce softmax probabilities (TS from temporal kernels); HB fuses neural scores with rule arbitration. History is handled by counters or windows (RB, SMLP, HB), momentum or sequential mass (BF, DS), and lag features (XGB); temporal processing mirrors this with debounce, prior momentum, sequential combination, lag encoding, flattened windows, 1D kernels, and smoothing. Safety is deterministic for RB and HB, evidence driven for BF and DS, and guarded for learned models. Only SMLP and HB learn online, TS uses synthetic pretrain plus fine tune, XGB retrains offline, and RB, BF, DS are static. Gradients drive XGB and the neural models; RB, BF, DS use none.

4.1 Experimental Setup  
Seven decision modules (Rule-Based: RB, Bayesian: BF, Dempster-Shafer: DF, XGBoost: XGB, SmallMLP: SMLP, Temporal Sequence: TS, and Hybrid: HB) were evaluated in a deterministic CAN-bus simulation across 15 scenarios (Figure 2): parking (S1), gradual approach (S2 - S4), sudden obstacles (S5A - S5E), and complex multi-object motion (S6A - S6F). Scenario rows report per-scenario F1 alongside an Expected performance target; aggregate rows give macro Precision, Recall, F1, Full_Brake false negative rate (FNR), mean time to decision (MTTD), and near-miss rate (NMR). All inputs were timestamp aligned and replayed identically across modules.

4.2 Functional Performance Analysis  
4.2.1 Classification Performance  
SmallMLP attains the highest average F1 (0.702) and leads most scenarios (S1-S4, S6A, S6B, S6E, S6F), combining stability across complexity with limited degradation under sudden transitions. Hybrid (0.686) and XGBoost (0.657) form the next tier; Rule-Based (0.653) and Dempster-Shafer (0.643) are mid range; Bayesian (0.615) underperforms; Sequence collapses (0.479). Leadership in sudden obstacle sectors is fragmented (XGB in S5A and S5D, HB in S5B and S5C, DF in S5E). In complex motion (S6), SMLP remains consistently high (0.721 - 0.947); HB peaks in S6C (0.921); XGB surges in S6D (0.850); TS deteriorates (?0.401). Net: SMLP is the preferred single model; HB and XGB offer situational uplift; DF is niche; RB and BF are fallback; TS is unsuitable.

4.2.2 Decision-Specific Performance  
Precision ranking: SMLP 0.679, HB 0.652, XGB 0.630, RB 0.616, DF 0.599, BF 0.577, TS 0.450. Recall ranking: HB 0.742, XGB 0.737, RB 0.731, SMLP 0.726, DF 0.698, BF 0.672, TS 0.608. Full_Brake FNR stratifies safety: SMLP 0.008 (best), TS 0.018, XGB 0.020, HB 0.022, DF 0.098, RB 0.102, BF 0.136 (elevated risk). SMLP should anchor precision critical decisions; HB added when recall coverage must be maximized; XGB applied for rapid motion evolution; DF only where evidential conflict dominates; RB and BF constrained to low complexity contexts; TS excluded.

4.3 Model-Specific Analysis  
Rule-Based provides the fastest response (MTTD 0.146) but a safety gap (FNR 0.102); retain as deterministic fallback. Bayesian exhibits early-scene fragility (S1 F1 0.104) and the highest braking miss rate (FNR 0.136); limit to gradual accumulation scenarios after calibration.

4.4 Cross-Scenario Performance Analysis  
4.4.1 Action Variations  
Precision stability (SMLP) and recall capture (HB and XGB) reflect complementary roles. Safety critical braking favors SMLP, then XGB and HB. False positives under Partial_Brake and Slowdown rise in lower precision models; TS is unreliable under temporal and spatial complexity.

4.4.2 Scenario Complexity Impact  
Parking (S1) penalizes Bayesian; SMLP remains near target. Gradual motion (S2 - S4) compresses gaps while TS is already weak. Sudden obstacles (S5) require specialization; no universal leader. Complex trajectories (S6) amplify divergence: SMLP holds, HB augments, XGB peaks selectively, TS collapses. Layer SMLP plus HB (and XGB when motion dynamics dominate) and add DF only for conflict heavy periphery ambiguity.

4.5 Non-Functional Performance  
4.5.1 Response Latency  
MTTD ordering from fast to slow: RB 0.146, BF 0.182, DF 0.192, SMLP 0.213, HB 0.222, TS 0.264, XGB 0.266. SMLP and HB offer acceptable trade offs; XGB may require pairing with faster arbitration for abrupt hazards.

4.5.2 Safety Reliability  
Full_Brake FNR confirms SMLP primacy (0.008). TS, XGB, HB (?0.022) are acceptable; DF, RB, BF (?0.098) demand gating. Critical braking logic should privilege SMLP with HB support; XGB admissible under motion emphasis; DF, RB, BF restricted.

4.5.3 Hazard Sensitivity  
Near-miss rate (lower better): XGB 53.83, SMLP 55.73, HB 62.57, BF 68.23, DF 69.53, TS 74.40, RB 77.67. Combine SMLP (precision plus safety) with XGB (hazard surfacing) and optionally HB (recall lift) for balanced risk coverage.

4.6 Statistical Significance  
Magnitude of observed separations (F1: SMLP 0.702 vs BF 0.615; FNR: 0.008 vs 0.136; latency: 0.146 vs 0.266; NMR: 53.83 vs 77.67) is operationally consequential even without interval estimates. Final deployment guidance: adopt SmallMLP as core; overlay Hybrid where missed detections are intolerable; insert XGBoost for dynamic hazard evolution; reserve Dempster-Shafer for ambiguous conflict zones; constrain Rule-Based and Bayesian to controlled fallback; exclude Sequence from production.

5. Conclusions
This study evaluated decision-level sensor fusion on the CAN bus using one standardized workflow to identify methods that balance accuracy, responsiveness, and safety. Across fifteen deterministic scenarios, SmallMLP achieved the highest F1 with fast detection in complex motion. SmHybrid minimized Full_Brake misses without added latency. XGBoost emphasized recall in movement heavy scenes but tended to trade precision. Dempster-Shafer performed well for braking under conflicting evidence but showed blind spots. Bayesian and rules were inconsistent, and the Sequence model repeatedly missed Slowdown, so it is unsuitable. Intermediate actions remained the dominant error source.
   Theoretically, the results support decision-level fusion as an auditable alternative to end-to-end stacks, validate lightweight neural models on structured signals, and show the value of deterministic safety overrides. For organisational management and workplace policy, the digital twin style pipeline with common inputs and outputs, timing fidelity, and safety metrics such as FNR and MTTD enables model risk management, compliance ready logging, and repeatable certification by distributed teams. In the post-pandemic context, these practices improve evidence-based decisions for updates and releases.
   Limitations include reliance on simulation, restricted sensing modalities, and class imbalance for intermediate actions. Future work should perform on-road validation across diverse environments, extend sensing to radar and infrared, apply cost sensitive and curriculum training with augmentation to sharpen intermediate decisions, and formalize runtime monitoring with human in the loop escalation and response time service levels. 
   Practitioners can choose SmallMLP for overall good performance, choose SmHybrid when minimizing Full_Brake misses is the priority, and choose XGBoost for movement heavy scenes.
   By coupling compact neural fusion with explicit safety logic in an auditable workflow, this study offers a scalable template for remote, standards driven engineering. The approach supports faster iteration, safer deployment, and higher productivity for distributed teams in the modern economy today.
